General Questions
What exactly is AURUM?
+
AURUM is a private market intelligence and paper-trading system. A funnel screens the market for assets the system can actually read. The Governor runs your algorithm suite on each one and combines the results into a composite score. A gate decides what is worth trading. A position monitor then manages every open trade around the clock — stops, trailing stops and timeouts — whether or not the app is open. Scoring can run on pure arithmetic (Deterministic, free) or with Claude reviewing each candidate.
When does AURUM generate picks?
+
Two ways. FILL TO 10 ONCE on the Simulator runs one scan immediately. AUTONOMOUS keeps the book filled while the app is open: it checks every 5 minutes, but only rescans when a position has closed or an hour has passed, so it does not burn scans finding nothing. Get Claude Trade Suggestion still gives a single on-demand pick.
Do I get a weekly email brief?
+
Not currently. AURUM does not send emails. All intelligence lives inside the app — check the Brief page for your current picks, watchlist, and accuracy stats anytime.
What does the composite score mean?
+
A 0-100 score from the Governor, weighting every algorithm that actually ran. 50 is neutral. Direction comes from a band set in the scoring contract: crypto goes LONG above 55 and SHORT below 45; stocks need above 60 or below 40. Anything between is NONE — no edge, no trade. Most candidates land between 45 and 55, which is why most scans reject more than they open. Algorithms with no data source are left out of the score entirely rather than counted as a neutral 50, which used to drag every score toward the middle.
How does AURUM grade its own accuracy?
+
Every pick is logged at the moment it's made. 30 days later, AURUM automatically compares the pick's actual return against the S&P 500 (SPY) over the same window — this runs quietly in the background whenever you open the Admin page. If the pick beat SPY, it's marked a win. The accuracy percentage on the Report page reflects this verified track record.
What does AURUM cost to run?
+
Deterministic mode makes no AI call, so scanning in it is free. Market data runs on free tiers, with the API keys held server-side by a proxy function rather than in the app. Hosting is GitHub Pages and the database is Supabase’s free tier. The only real cost is Claude, and only in CLAUDE or BOTH scoring mode — roughly one call per candidate scored.
Do I need a broker account to use AURUM?
+
No. The Simulator works entirely with paper money tracked in your own database — no broker, no ID verification, no signup required. A live broker connection (currently Alpaca, on the Brokerage page) is only needed if you eventually want AURUM to place real trades automatically. Until then, you can manually execute any Claude-suggested trade through whatever brokerage app you already use.
Operating the Simulator
Which scoring mode should I use?
+
DETERMINISTIC for routine scanning. It is free, gives the same answer for the same data, and on your own results it predicted outcomes better than Claude’s composite did. CLAUDE adds a model review of every candidate, at a cost per call. BOTH runs Claude but records the deterministic score alongside it, so you can measure the difference.
What do the scale presets do?
+
They set how many positions and how large. Conservative: 10 positions at 5% each, half your balance working. Scaled: 30 at 3%. Aggressive: 50 at 2%, where fixed costs start to bite. A bigger preset does not mean more picks: the funnel shortlists about 25 candidates and only some clear the gate, so Aggressive usually opens the same few names at a smaller size. Conservative is the recommended default, and the one to use for Swing mode.
What is the difference between FILL TO 10 ONCE and AUTONOMOUS?
+
FILL TO 10 ONCE runs one scan now and stops. AUTONOMOUS repeats on its own while the app is open — it checks every 5 minutes but only rescans after a position closes or an hour passes. Both use the same funnel, gate and sizing. Neither opens positions with the app closed: opening needs the app, managing does not.
What is the scan log?
+
The box under the scan buttons. It keeps the last 80 lines, newest first and timestamped: how many coins were screened, which of your watchlist coins sat outside the volatility band, how each wave scored, and why candidates failed. Read it whenever a scan opens fewer positions than you expect. CLEAR empties it.
How do I copy my open positions?
+
Tap COPY ALL beside Open Positions. It copies every open trade as plain text — ticker, direction, quantity, entry, current price, stop, target, trailing status, volatility and CoinGecko id. The trail line shows either the exact price where the trailing stop will activate, or, once it is active, how far behind the price it is following and what it has locked in.
What do the System Diagnostics buttons do?
+
SCAN checks your live data: one portfolio row, every position priceable, P&L and cost basis correct, stops valid, balance reconciling. RUN BACKTEST tests the scoring against historical prices. CHECK OUTCOMES resolves 24-hour and 7-day results for scored candidates, and needs the outcome-checker function deployed. RECALIBRATE rebuilds derived figures from raw trade data — it cannot fix a pricing or stop problem, which needs a code change.
What do position alerts mean?
+
Notices from the position monitor: stop or target hit, stop trailed, stale timeout, swing reversal, or a price it could not fetch. They show in every scoring mode, in a scrollable box with a count and a DELETE ALL button, each labelled and colour-coded: green when a stop has been raised or profit banked (STOP TRAILED, TRAILING STOP, TARGET REACHED), gold for a swing (SWING REVERSAL), red for a loss. These are how the system tells you where your stops are moving and when to swing. Momentary “price unavailable” misses are left out, since the monitor retries and falls back on its own — System Diagnostics → SCAN checks for any position that genuinely can’t be priced.
How Picks Are Chosen
How does AURUM choose what to scan?
+
The funnel screens the top 1,000 coins and keeps only what the algorithms can read: a daily range between 1% and 4.5%, market cap above $150M, and 24-hour volume above $25M. It ranks the survivors by readability — closeness to a 3% daily range (45%), liquidity (25%), size (20%) — with movement only as a tiebreak (10%). It used to rank by how much a coin was moving, which selected exactly the chaotic, thin assets that lost.
Why a volatility band?
+
Volatility was the strongest predictor in your own data. Across scored candidates, a daily range of 2.5-3.5% won about 70% of the time; 4.5-6% won 31%; above 6%, 14-17%. Volatile coins also need wider stops, so every loss is larger. Within calm coins the composite score predicts outcomes; across all coins it barely does. The band points the algorithms at assets they can read. Caution: this was measured over a short period in a falling market.
What gets excluded entirely?
+
Stablecoins; tokenized stocks such as bStocks and xStocks (SNDKB was SanDisk wrapped as a token); gold-backed tokens such as XAUT and PAXG; and tokenized loans and money-market funds such as FIGR_HELOC, BUIDL and USDY. They look like ideal calm candidates because they are pegged to something else — their charts describe gold or a loan book, not a crypto asset.
How does my watchlist fit in?
+
In crypto, your watchlist goes through the same filters and competes on the same score as everything the funnel finds — it no longer jumps the queue. Watchlist coins outside the volatility band sit out that scan and are named in the scan log; they come back whenever they are calm. Every candidate and trade records whether it came from your watchlist or the funnel, so the two can be compared on outcomes. Stock mode still puts the watchlist first.
What decides whether a scored candidate is traded?
+
Three tests from the scoring contract, all of which must pass. Signal quality: crypto 45, stocks 60 — a floor that tested positive on your data. Direction: the composite must clear the band. Opportunity: crypto 50, stocks 70. In Swing mode, signal quality still applies; longs still need a bullish read, and shorts need a confirmed downtrend instead of a bearish composite.
How Positions Are Managed
How are stops and targets set?
+
From each asset’s own volatility. The stop sits about 2x its daily range from entry and the target at 2:1 — twice the stop distance. A coin moving 3% a day gets a stop about 6% away and a target about 12% away. Calmer coins get tighter levels, volatile ones wider.
What is the trailing stop?
+
Once a position is up half a day’s normal movement, its stop moves to breakeven, then follows the price half a day’s movement behind. It only ever tightens, so a position that has gained can no longer turn into a loss. On a coin moving 3.82% a day it activates at +1.91% and trails 1.91% behind. Shorts work the same way in reverse. It runs in every mode.
When does a position close for going nowhere?
+
The stale rule. Swing: after 168 hours (7 days) if it has moved less than 3%. Day: after 48 hours if under 1.5%. Positions in profit by 0.5% or more are exempt — a slow winner isn’t going nowhere. A backstop closes anything at 336 hours (two weeks) regardless, recorded as max_hold.
Are positions managed while the app is closed?
+
Yes. The position monitor runs server-side on a schedule and manages every open position in both directions: it prices them, raises trailing stops, applies the stale rule, runs Swing reviews, and closes on stop or target. Opening new positions needs the app; managing them does not.
How are positions priced?
+
From the exact CoinGecko id stored on each position when it opened, so any coin the funnel can open can be priced. If CoinGecko fails or rate-limits, the monitor retries once, then falls back to CryptoCompare. Fallback prices are checked against the entry — one more than 3x away is treated as a different coin sharing the ticker and rejected, rather than risking a false stop. If a cycle misses, the next one checks the price range it missed.
What do the close reasons mean?
+
target_reached — hit its target. stop_loss_triggered — hit its original stop, a loss. trailing_stop — gained, then turned, and the trailed stop closed it. stale_timeout — flat past the stale window. max_hold — reached two weeks. swing_review — closed by Swing mode to reverse. manual_close — closed by you.
Swing Mode — Long / Short
What is Swing mode?
+
A mode, off by default, switched with SWING on the Simulator. When on, scans open longs that are predicted to rise and shorts that are predicted to fall — up to 10 of each on the Conservative preset. And positions it opens can reverse: a long that tops out and turns can become a short. The point is to catch both the climb and the fall. When off, everything runs exactly as before, longs only.
How does it pick longs and shorts?
+
Each side on its own evidence. A long opens when the composite reads bullish — in Swing mode it needs 60 or more, since the 56–57 longs were the ones that hit their stops. A short opens when the coin is in a confirmed downtrend: price below its 50-day average, the 20-day average below the 50-day and falling, and a lower high. The composite is not used for shorts — across 350 scored candidates it read bearish once, and that coin rose 16.5%. A coin can never be on both sides.
What makes a good short?
+
Shorting the rally inside a downtrend, never the bottom. The best entry is a coin in a confirmed downtrend that has bounced back toward its 20-day average — that average acts as resistance. Each qualifying coin gets a setup score out of 100 from how far apart its averages are, how steeply the 20-day is falling, how close the price has rallied to resistance, and whether RSI sits in the 45-58 range a bounce typically reaches. The best setups open first.
Why are some falling coins never shorted?
+
Three things disqualify a short. Oversold — RSI under 35, where bounces come from. Capitulation — a day that fell more than two daily ranges. Stretched — more than two daily ranges below its 20-day average. Each of these is a coin that has already fallen hard, and in crypto those tend to bounce. Every short that lost money before was one of them. The scan log lists the most common reasons shorts were refused.
Why don’t I get equal longs and shorts?
+
Because the book follows the market rather than being forced into balance. In a rally most coins read bullish and few are in downtrends, so you get more longs; in a decline, the reverse. Forcing them equal used to mean shorting coins that weren’t falling. If a scan finds no genuine setup on one side, it opens nothing on that side.
Why are my Swing positions different sizes?
+
So every position carries the same risk, not just the same money. Each is sized inversely to its volatility: a coin moving 4% a day gets a smaller position than one moving 2.5%, so that hitting its stop costs the same. A coin moving 3% a day gets the standard 5%, and every size stays between half and double that. Only Swing mode sizes this way; the normal scan uses a flat 5%.
How do reversals work?
+
Never decided at entry, and never on a timer. A Swing position is left alone until it has captured capital — its trailing stop has reached breakeven — and then stalled: its trailing stop hasn’t moved for 8 hours, meaning the price has stopped making progress. Only then is it checked. It reverses only on a confirmed turn: price through its 20-day average, momentum on the new side, and the old trend no longer intact. A pause inside a healthy trend holds. If the trailing stop fires first, the same check runs at that moment. An original stop hit at a loss never reverses.
Won’t it reverse on every dip?
+
No — that is the hardest part to get right, and it was tested directly. A rule built on price and RSI alone fired on every pullback inside a rally and every bounce inside a decline. The deciding ingredient is that the old trend must have broken: an uptrend is intact while its 20-day average is above the 50-day and still rising. Across 96 simulated pullbacks and bounces, the rule used here made zero false reversals, while catching about 43% of genuine turns. The turns it misses aren’t lost — the trailing stop has already banked the gain.
Which positions can be reversed?
+
Only positions opened by a Swing-mode scan, and the reversals that come from them. Everything else in the book — including anything opened before Swing mode — runs to its stop, its target or its trailing stop and is never reversed.
Can it flip back and forth?
+
No. A position opened by a reversal starts with its trailing stop inactive, so it must capture capital of its own and then stall before it can be considered again.
Does Swing mode work with live trading?
+
No — it is paper only. Your wallet trades on a spot exchange, which cannot short, so Swing refuses to run while live trading is armed. Live shorts would need a perpetual futures venue.
How should I run a Swing test?
+
Disarm live trading, set scoring to DETERMINISTIC, keep the Conservative preset, turn SWING on, and tap FILL TO 10 ONCE. The scan log reports how many candidates read bullish, how many sat in a confirmed downtrend, how many of each it opened, and the main reasons shorts were refused. Each position’s note says why it opened — Long #1 of 6 bullish or Downtrend setup 84/100. Then let them run — closing them by hand records your decision, not the system’s.
What is the risk?
+
Shorts lose in a rally, and because the book follows the market it is not hedged: when most of what it holds is long, a broad drop hurts. The downtrend rule is standard practice but untested on your account — your earlier exports don’t include the moving averages needed to backtest it. Treat the first weeks as data, not results.
The Algorithms
How many algorithms does AURUM run, and what's the Governor?
+
33 are stored; each scan runs the ones with a real data source — typically 13 to 18 in crypto. The rest are left out entirely rather than scored a neutral 50: stock-only signals in crypto mode, signals with no connected feed, and two excluded permanently — attention_augmented_gru for its CPU cost and agentic_llm_workflow because it is not actionable. The most expensive algorithms can be skipped to stay inside the server’s CPU limit. The Governor runs the suite, weights each algorithm, and returns the composite and direction.
What does the Momentum Detector do?
+
Measures price velocity using RSI, MACD, and rate-of-change across multiple timeframes, confirmed by volume trends. A stock moving up fast with rising volume scores higher than one drifting up on thin volume.
What does the Sentiment Detector do?
+
Analyzes recent news headlines from Finnhub for positive and negative language, combined with analyst recommendation data, to produce a bullish, bearish, or neutral sentiment score.
What does the Political Signal Detector do?
+
Tracks congressional trading activity when connected to a data source. Members of Congress buying a stock is treated as a meaningful bullish signal. This algorithm runs at neutral until a congressional trading data key is added.
What does the Options Flow Detector do?
+
Detects unusual options activity — sweeps, dark pool flow, and call/put volume imbalances. This is AURUM's highest-weighted algorithm in the Governor (2.2x) because options flow is one of the most reliable early signals of institutional positioning. Runs at neutral until an options flow data source is connected.
What does the Insider Transaction Tracker do?
+
Tracks insider buying and selling via Finnhub. Executives purchasing their own company's stock with personal funds is weighted heavily (2.0x in the Governor) — insiders rarely buy unless they believe the stock is undervalued.
What does the Macro Regime Detector do?
+
Identifies the current macroeconomic regime — goldilocks, low-rate expansion, high-rate expansion, recession, stagflation, risk-off, and more — and tells the Governor which algorithms to trust most in that environment. In a recession, macro and correlation signals get amplified; momentum gets dampened.
What is the Governor's signal quality score?
+
How much trustworthy data stood behind a read. In Deterministic mode it is computed from how many algorithms ran. It measures data sources, not conviction — and on your data, candidates below 45 lost money while those at 60 and above won most of the time. It is a gate in every mode.
How do I improve an algorithm?
+
Go to the Admin panel → Algorithm Improvement section. Select the algorithm you want to change. Describe the improvement in plain English — for example "The momentum detector flags too many small cap stocks. I only want large cap above $10B market cap." AURUM rewrites the code and presents it for your approval before anything deploys. You never need to touch code directly.
Crypto Mode
How do I switch to Crypto mode?
+
Tap the STOCKS / CRYPTO toggle in the header of the Brief page. The switch persists — every page in the app reads your active mode on load and filters all data accordingly. Your stock watchlist and crypto watchlist stay completely separate.
Which algorithms run in Crypto mode?
+
In Crypto mode, 4 stock-specific algorithms are replaced with crypto equivalents, and 2 entirely new algorithms are added that have no stock equivalent:

Replacements: crypto_derivatives_flow (2.2x) replaces options_flow_detector, crypto_whale_tracker (2.0x) replaces insider_transaction_tracker, crypto_protocol_events (1.3x) replaces earnings_cycle_detector, crypto_regulatory_signals (0.7x) replaces political_signal_detector.

New: crypto_onchain_momentum (1.8x) — NVT ratio, MVRV Z-score, SOPR, active addresses. crypto_market_cycle_detector (1.6x) — halving cycle phase, BTC dominance, Fear & Greed Index. All ML/AI algorithms (phases 3-4) run unchanged in both modes.
How do I execute crypto trades?
+
No broker, no ID required. Claude generates a complete DEX execution instruction with every crypto trade suggestion — which DEX to use, the trading pair, and the exact steps. Execute via Uniswap (ERC-20 tokens like ETH, LINK, UNI), dYdX (BTC and ETH perpetuals with leverage), or Jupiter (Solana-based tokens like SOL). Connect your MetaMask or Coinbase Wallet and follow the instruction exactly.
Can AURUM trade both directions in crypto?
+
Yes. In the normal scan a candidate goes SHORT when its composite falls below 45, but that is rare — composites cluster around 52, and shorts taken on a low absolute score have lost. Swing mode is the way to trade both directions: it opens shorts only on a confirmed downtrend read from price structure, and refuses coins that are oversold, capitulating or stretched. Live shorts need a perpetual futures venue; the spot wallet cannot short.
How much of the crypto intelligence works for free?
+
Deterministic scoring is entirely free. Connected free sources: CoinGecko prices, history and global data; Binance funding rates where a perpetual exists; the Fear & Greed Index; and Finnhub news. Several algorithms have no source yet — options flow, exchange whale flows, macro — and are left out of the score rather than guessed at.
What are crypto regimes and how do they work?
+
The Governor detects 10 crypto-specific market regimes — bull_run, altseason, accumulation, distribution, markdown, pre_halving, post_halving, extreme_fear, extreme_greed, and regulatory_event. Each regime re-weights the algorithms the Governor trusts most. In a bull_run, momentum and derivatives flow get amplified. In accumulation, on-chain metrics and whale tracking dominate. In extreme_fear, the Governor applies contrarian signals — the same way crypto funds trade the Fear & Greed Index as a reversal indicator.
How do I connect my wallet for crypto trading?
+
On the Trading page in crypto mode, tap Connect Wallet for DEX Execution in the auto-fill panel. AURUM detects MetaMask or Coinbase Wallet automatically via your browser. Once connected, your shortened wallet address appears on the button confirming the connection. You need funds in your wallet to execute live trades — USDC or ETH for Uniswap, USDC for dYdX perpetuals.
Does AURUM monitor my trades when the app is closed?
+
Yes. The position monitor runs server-side and manages every open position in both directions: it prices them from their stored CoinGecko id, raises trailing stops, applies the stale rule, runs Swing reviews when that mode is on, and closes on stop or target. Open a position, close the app, and the system keeps managing it.
What runs automatically while the app is offline?
+
Server-side, on schedule: the Position Monitor prices every open trade, raises trailing stops, applies the stale rule, runs Swing reviews and closes on stop or target. The Strategy Monitor compares Day and Swing results. The news job fetches headlines for your watchlist. Opening new positions still needs the app. The Diagnostic page’s Workers section shows what each one last wrote.
Data & Diagnostics
What is the Diagnostic page?
+
A separate page, diagnostic.html, that checks the system end to end: database tables and columns, the scoring contract, write permissions, trade logic, the algorithms, the code your Simulator is actually running, every edge function, the scheduled workers, the Governor in every mode, a full traced workflow, the data pipeline and the price feeds. Run sections one at a time — Workers, Governor, Live code and Workflow are the most useful. Keep write tests on; turn Claude paths on only when you want to spend credits.
What gets recorded for analysis?
+
Every scored candidate goes to shadow_log, whether it opened or not — its scores, direction, volatility, source and pipeline version. That is what calibration reads, not your trades. So the data builds up from scans, not from how many positions you hold, and deterministic scans add it for free.
Should I close or delete a trade I don’t want in my record?
+
Closing records a finished trade and counts toward your win rate. Deleting removes it as if it never happened. If a position was opened by mistake or by a bug, delete it rather than close it. Don’t delete genuine losses just because they lost — then your win rate stops meaning anything.
Features & Pages
How do I add stocks to the watchlist?
+
Go to Admin → Manage Watchlist. Enter the ticker, company name, and sector. Watchlist tickers feed the price bar on the Brief and Trading pages, and are available as targets when generating a Governor-backed trade instruction. Quality over quantity — a focused watchlist produces sharper intelligence.
How does the Paper Trading Simulator work?
+
It trades $10,000 of simulated capital with no broker and no real money. Pick a scoring mode and a preset, then run FILL TO 10 ONCE or AUTONOMOUS. Each opened position gets a volatility-sized stop and a 2:1 target, then the position monitor manages it — trailing stop, stale rule and, in Swing mode, reversals. Closed trades feed your win rate and equity curve. See Operating the Simulator above for every control.
How does the News Feed work?
+
The News page displays headlines stored in AURUM's database, scored for sentiment and cross-referenced with your watchlist. Sentiment scoring for individual tickers is generated live by the Sentiment Detector algorithm whenever the Governor runs, using real-time headlines pulled from Finnhub.
How do Price Alerts work?
+
Go to Admin → Price Alerts. Select a watchlist stock, choose whether you want to alert on upward or downward movement, set your percentage threshold, and enter your entry price. AURUM monitors and flags when the threshold is reached. Alerts are visible in the Admin panel and can be removed anytime.
What does the Sectors page show?
+
The Sectors page groups your watchlist stocks by sector — Technology, Finance, Energy, etc. — and shows the average AURUM score per sector. This helps you see where AURUM's collective intelligence is most bullish and whether your watchlist is too concentrated in one sector. The score bar shows relative strength visually.
How does the Trading page work?
+
Choose Stock or Options, Day or Swing strategy, and Manual or Auto mode. Tap Generate Instruction — the Governor runs all 25 algorithms with live data, then Claude returns a complete trade instruction. Manual mode shows five ranked trades to choose from. Auto mode autofills a single highest-conviction trade, ready to execute through your connected broker.
What does the Options page do?
+
Tracks open options positions and gives Claude-powered assessments — TAKE PROFIT, HOLD, FLIP, or EXIT — based on current price, days to expiration, and AURUM's latest signal alignment for that ticker.
What is the Pick History Archive?
+
The History page logs every pick AURUM has ever made — score, brief, signal breakdown, date, and outcome. Filter by All, Real, Simulated, or Score 80+. This is your verified track record — the foundation of AURUM's credibility over time. Nothing is deleted or hidden.
Glossary of Terms
Composite Score
The Governor’s 0-100 score after weighting every algorithm that actually ran. 50 is neutral. Direction comes from the scoring contract’s band — crypto LONG above 55, SHORT below 45.
Signal Quality
How much trustworthy data stood behind a score. Gates every trade: crypto needs 45, stocks 60. Measures data sources, not conviction.
Signal Conflict
When AURUM's algorithms disagree significantly — a wide spread between bullish and bearish scores, or high standard deviation across results. Flagged as moderate or severe conflict, reducing the Governor's confidence rating.
Smart Money
Institutional investors, hedge funds, and corporate insiders whose positioning often predicts price movement. Options flow and insider transactions are AURUM's two highest-weighted smart money signals in the Governor.
Macro Regime
The current macroeconomic environment — goldilocks, low-rate expansion, high-rate expansion, recession, stagflation, risk-off, deflation risk, or high-volatility bear. The Governor re-weights which algorithms it trusts most based on the detected regime.
Sentiment Velocity
The rate of change in sentiment — not just whether it's positive but whether it's accelerating or decelerating. Accelerating positive sentiment is a stronger signal than peaked sentiment.
Self-Rating
AURUM's system for grading its own accuracy. Picks are logged at entry and automatically compared against the S&P 500's return 30 days later. Runs quietly in the background each time the Admin page loads.
Beat Market
A pick that outperformed the S&P 500 (SPY) return over the same 30-day period. The benchmark used by AURUM's self-rating system and shown on the Report page.
Correlation Risk
When two or more watchlist stocks move almost identically. High correlation means your watchlist provides less diversification than it appears.
Earnings Cycle
The period around a company's quarterly earnings report. The Governor applies a confidence penalty to signals within the earnings window due to elevated binary-event risk.
Options Flow
The volume and direction of options contracts being traded, including sweeps and dark pool activity. Unusual call volume suggests bullish smart money positioning; unusual put volume suggests bearish positioning. AURUM's highest-weighted algorithm (2.2x).
Governor
The 26th algorithm — the master orchestrator. Runs after all 25 others, weighting and synthesizing their outputs into one composite score, verdict, and signal quality rating before Claude ever sees the data.
Paper Trading
Simulated trading with virtual money, tracked entirely in AURUM's own database — no broker or ID required. AURUM's simulator starts with $10,000 in simulated funds.
DEX (Decentralized Exchange)
A peer-to-peer trading platform that operates without a central authority. Uniswap, dYdX, and Jupiter are the three DEXs AURUM recommends for trade execution. No ID, no broker account — just a crypto wallet.
Funding Rate
A periodic payment between long and short traders on perpetual futures contracts. Extreme positive funding means too many longs — a contrarian sell signal. Extreme negative funding means too many shorts — a contrarian buy signal. AURUM's crypto_derivatives_flow algorithm is built around this signal.
MVRV Z-Score
Market Value to Realized Value Z-Score. Measures how far the current market price deviates from the average price all coins last moved at. Below zero historically marks major bottoms. Above 7 historically marks major tops. One of the most powerful single signals in crypto_onchain_momentum.
SOPR
Spent Output Profit Ratio. Measures whether coins moved on-chain are moving at a profit or loss. SOPR below 1 means coins are moving at a loss — historically a capitulation and buy signal.
NVT Ratio
Network Value to Transactions ratio — crypto's equivalent of the P/E ratio. High NVT means the network is valued more than its transaction activity justifies (overvalued). Low NVT means undervalued relative to usage.
BTC Dominance
Bitcoin's percentage of total cryptocurrency market capitalization. Rising dominance signals risk-off behavior and bear market conditions. Falling dominance signals capital rotating into altcoins — a late bull market indicator tracked by crypto_market_cycle_detector.
Halving Cycle
Bitcoin's block reward halves approximately every 4 years, reducing new supply. Historically each halving triggers a bull market 12-18 months later. AURUM's crypto_market_cycle_detector tracks which phase of the halving cycle the market is currently in.
Fear & Greed Index
Alternative.me's 0-100 sentiment score for the crypto market. 0-25 = Extreme Fear (contrarian buy). 75-100 = Extreme Greed (contrarian sell). AURUM fetches this free, no key required, and feeds it directly into crypto_market_cycle_detector.
Altseason
A period when altcoins (non-Bitcoin cryptocurrencies) significantly outperform Bitcoin. Characterized by falling BTC dominance and rising total market cap. The Governor applies the altseason regime weight matrix when these conditions are detected, amplifying momentum and sentiment signals.
Exchange Flow
The movement of crypto assets into and out of centralized exchanges. Coins moving INTO exchanges suggest holders are preparing to sell (bearish). Coins moving OUT suggests accumulation (bullish). Tracked by crypto_whale_tracker — requires Glassnode or CryptoQuant for full data.
A message included in the Governor's output whenever an algorithm fails, is skipped due to missing data, or a key data source (like options flow) isn't connected. Claude is told exactly what didn't run so it can account for the gap.
Plug and Play
AURUM's architecture for data sources. The integration code is already built for each algorithm — adding an API key (Twelve Data, Finnhub, Unusual Whales, Quiver) activates real data immediately without any additional coding.
MetaMask / Coinbase Wallet
Browser-based crypto wallets that connect directly to DEXs. No account, no ID, no broker. AURUM detects these automatically via window.ethereum when you tap Connect Wallet on the Trading page.
Position Monitor
The server-side function (quick-service) that manages open positions on a schedule: prices them, trails stops, applies the stale rule, runs Swing reviews, and closes on stop or target — app open or not.
Deterministic Mode
Scoring by weighted arithmetic over the algorithm suite, with no AI call. Free and reproducible.
Scoring Contract
The single stored set of thresholds — signal quality, direction and opportunity floors for crypto and stocks — that every part of the system reads.
Funnel
The screen that narrows the market to a shortlist worth scoring, by volatility, size and liquidity.
Volatility Band
The daily-range window the funnel accepts: 1% to 4.5%, ranked toward 3%. Where the algorithms read best.
Trailing Stop
A stop that moves to breakeven once a position is up half a day’s movement, then follows the price half a day’s movement behind. Only ever tightens.
Breakeven
A stop placed at the entry price, so the position can no longer close at a loss.
Stale Timeout
Closes a position that has gone nowhere: 7 days and under 3% for swing, 2 days and under 1.5% for day. Positions in profit are exempt.
Max Hold
The two-week backstop that closes any position regardless, so capital is never tied up indefinitely.
Capital Captured
A position whose trailing stop has reached breakeven or beyond. A Swing position is only checked for a reversal once it is captured and has stalled.
Swing Mode
The toggleable mode that opens longs predicted to rise and shorts in a confirmed downtrend, each on its own evidence, and reverses a Swing position when it stalls and the move genuinely turns. Paper only.
Swing Reversal
Closing a Swing position that has captured capital and stalled, and opening the opposite one, when the move has genuinely turned and the old trend has broken.
Oversold (RSI)
A coin whose 14-day RSI is under 30 — usually near the bottom of a fall. Never shorted, because such coins tend to bounce.
Shadow Log
The record of every scored candidate, opened or not. What calibration and outcome analysis read.
Source
Whether a candidate or trade came from your watchlist or the funnel, so the two can be compared on results.
Scale Preset
Conservative, Scaled or Aggressive — how many positions and what size each.
Downtrend Setup
What a short needs: price below its 50-day average, the 20-day below the 50-day and falling, a lower high, and the price not oversold, capitulating or stretched. Scored out of 100.
Stall
When a position’s trailing stop hasn’t moved for 8 hours — the price has stopped making progress. The moment Swing mode checks for a turn.
Trend Broken
An uptrend whose 20-day average is no longer above the 50-day and rising, or the mirror for a downtrend. Required before any reversal, so pullbacks and bounces are never mistaken for turns.
